Technology Risks

Rights and Informed Consent

Current deepfake technology can already generate an actor’s face, voice, and behavior out of thin air—without the actor being present or even aware of it.

Taking the research by Murphy and others as an example, the biggest concern among respondents was: What should be done if a production team uses an actor’s likeness in a performance without permission, or uses it without providing reasonable compensation?

The Impact of Misinformation on Audience Trust

Although deepfakes in movies are mostly intended for entertainment, if they are released out of context or without clear labeling, audiences can easily be left unsure of what is real. Research has found that watching AI-generated re-enactments can cause some viewers to form false memories.

The good news, however, is that studies also show deepfakes’ ability to distort memories is actually no more pronounced than that of text-only descriptions.

Employment in the Industry & WGA/SAG-AFTRA Concerns

During the Hollywood strikes of the past two years, one of the central points of contention was AI taking away jobs. If major studios were to use AI stand-ins to replace real actors, many entry-level actors and behind-the-scenes crew members would likely lose their jobs outright.

More importantly, many viewers are averse to artificial, virtual performances because they feel this undermines the actors’ ability to convey genuine emotions and destroys the authentic artistic soul of the film.
